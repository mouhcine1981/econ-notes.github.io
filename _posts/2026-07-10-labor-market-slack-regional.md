The national unemployment rate has held in a narrow band for most of this year, which has led to a general read that the labor market is holding up well. That headline number is masking meaningful regional divergence.

## Three regions, three different stories

Grouping states by their dominant industry exposure over the last four employment reports shows a clear split:

- **Manufacturing-exposed states** have seen job growth flatten, consistent with the ongoing inventory correction in durable goods.
- **Services-heavy metro areas** continue to add jobs at close to their pre-pandemic trend pace.
- **Government-employment-heavy states** are the one category showing outright softening, tied to state-level budget tightening cycles that don't show up cleanly in national payroll data.

## Why the aggregate number hides this

National payroll figures are a population-weighted average, so a large services sector can numerically offset real softening elsewhere. For a policymaker or allocator, the aggregate print is the wrong resolution to be looking at if the question is "where is the economy actually turning."
