Most criticism of central bank communication focuses on forward guidance: does the dot plot help or hurt, is "data dependent" a meaningful phrase or a way to avoid commitment. That's a real debate, but it's not the communication gap that matters most for practitioners right now.

The bigger gap is between the **committee's stated reaction function** and the **market's inferred reaction function** — and the two have been drifting apart for several consecutive meetings.

## Two ways to read the same statement

A hawkish read of recent statement language points to the repeated emphasis on "sufficiently restrictive." A dovish read points to the softened language around the balance of risks. Both readings are defensible from the same text — which is itself the problem. When a statement supports two opposite market narratives equally well, it has stopped doing the job forward guidance is supposed to do.

## What this means in practice

For practitioners, the practical takeaway isn't a prediction about the next move — it's a risk-management point: **position sizing around single-meeting statement reactions should account for this ambiguity being structural, not a temporary communication lapse.**
