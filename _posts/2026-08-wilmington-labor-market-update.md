*This is a short-form summary of our full policy brief, [available as a PDF here]({{ '/papers/2026-08-nc-wilmington-labor-markets.pdf' | relative_url }}).*

North Carolina's labor market keeps outperforming the nation. Total nonfarm employment stood 11.3% above its pre-pandemic (June 2019) baseline as of June 2026, ranking 7th among all states and D.C. Wilmington, in turn, has outpaced the state in most recent years, and the region's growth compounds on top of an already strong state performance.

![NC metro areas ranked by employment growth, June 2025 to 2026]({{ '/assets/images/A4_nc_metro_growth_june2026.png' | relative_url }})

## Wilmington has rarely left the top tier

Wilmington ranked 4th of 15 NC metros for employment growth in the most recent year (+1.67%), and it has never ranked worse than 4th of 15 in any of the last four years — after being the single fastest-growing NC metro outright in 2021–2022. Its post-COVID average annual growth pace, 4.14%, exceeds its pre-COVID average of 1.90% by 2.24 percentage points, the second-largest such acceleration among NC metros, behind only Pinehurst-Southern Pines.

![Wilmington's employment growth rank among NC metros over time]({{ '/assets/images/A1_wilmington_rank_over_time.png' | relative_url }})

## Healthcare, not tourism, is driving Wilmington's growth

Statewide, Mining, Logging & Construction and Financial Activities have posted the largest cumulative employment gains since 2019. Wilmington's own growth composition looks different: Education & Health Services has grown 72.3% since 2019 in the region, more than double any other local sector, and far outpaces its position (4th) at the state level. Government is Wilmington's only sector with negative cumulative growth (-13.8%), largely a reflection of the local hospital sale shifting jobs from Government into Education & Health Services on the books.

![Wilmington employment growth by sector since 2019]({{ '/assets/images/A2_wilmington_sector_growth_since_2019.png' | relative_url }})

## Wages tell a more uneven story

Wilmington has posted the fastest cumulative wage growth of any NC metro since 2019 — average weekly earnings rose 52%, from $742 to $1,129. But that long-run strength masks a rougher recent year: over the most recent 12 months, Wilmington's average weekly wage actually fell 1.0% even as the statewide average rose 1.8%. Wilmington still ranks only 5th of 15 NC metros by current wage *level*, so the region has closed a lot of ground since 2019 without yet catching up.

![Wilmington's cumulative wage growth vs. other NC metros since 2019]({{ '/assets/images/A3_wilmington_wage_growth_vs_metros.png' | relative_url }})

## Why it matters

Wilmington's labor market has genuinely re-accelerated since the pandemic, and it is doing so on the back of healthcare rather than the tourism and construction growth the region is usually associated with. But the same period shows wage growth and employment growth pulling in different directions in the most recent year: jobs keep coming, yet the area's paychecks briefly lost ground to the rest of the state. That combination — strong structural job growth, a wobblier near-term wage picture — is worth keeping in mind when reading either number in isolation.
